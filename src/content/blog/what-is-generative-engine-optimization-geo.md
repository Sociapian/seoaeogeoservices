When someone asks ChatGPT "which agency should I hire for SEO in Delhi?" or asks Perplexity to compare two products, the answer usually names a handful of brands and links to a few sources. **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** is the work of making sure your brand is one of them.

This guide explains what GEO is, how AI engines choose what to mention, and the practical steps we use with clients at Sociapa.

## What is GEO?

Generative Engine Optimization is the practice of improving how often, and how accurately, AI assistants mention and cite your brand. It covers tools that generate answers instead of showing a list of links, including:

- **ChatGPT** (with search)
- **Google Gemini** and **Google AI Overviews** in Search
- **Perplexity**
- **Microsoft Copilot**

The goal is not a ranking position. It is to be **named in the answer** and **cited as a source**, so the person asking trusts you before they have even visited your site.

## How do AI engines decide which brands to mention?

Most AI assistants answer questions about products, services and companies in two stages:

1. **Retrieval.** When the assistant searches the web, it pulls a set of pages that look relevant, much like a search engine does. If your page cannot be crawled or does not rank for the topic, it is unlikely to be retrieved.
2. **Synthesis.** The model reads the retrieved pages, plus what it learned during training, and writes an answer. It tends to mention brands that appear consistently across several trustworthy sources and that are described clearly.

That is why GEO and SEO are so closely linked. Google itself says the same SEO best practices apply to its AI features: pages need to be indexed, crawlable and genuinely helpful. GEO adds a layer on top that focuses on clarity, evidence and reputation.

## GEO vs SEO: what changes?

| | SEO | GEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Rank a page in search results | Be named and cited inside an AI answer |
| Unit of success | A position and a click | A mention, a citation, an accurate description |
| Main levers | Technical health, content, links | Entity clarity, citable facts, third-party mentions, crawl access |
| How it is measured | Rankings, traffic, conversions | Share of AI answers, citations, referral traffic from AI tools |

## Seven practical steps to improve your GEO

### 1. Make sure AI crawlers can read your site

Check your `robots.txt`. If it blocks crawlers such as `GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot`, `ClaudeBot` or `PerplexityBot`, those systems cannot read your pages. Decide deliberately which ones to allow. Many brands block AI crawlers by accident through a security plugin or a copied robots file.

Also check that your key content is in the HTML itself. Many AI crawlers do not run JavaScript, so content that only appears after scripts load may be invisible to them.

### 2. Be unmistakable about who you are

AI models connect mentions to an **entity**: your brand as a distinct thing. Help them by:

- Using the same brand name, description, address and contact details everywhere.
- Adding **Organization** structured data with your logo, address, contact point and `sameAs` links to your official profiles (LinkedIn, Instagram, Google Business Profile, Crunchbase and so on).
- Having a clear About page that says what you do, for whom, and where.

### 3. Write answer-first content

Assistants quote content that answers a question directly. For each important topic, start with a two or three sentence answer, then go into detail. Use headings phrased as the questions your buyers ask, and use lists and tables where they make the answer clearer.

### 4. Publish facts worth citing

Models prefer sources that contain something concrete: a definition, a process, a price range, a comparison, original data from your own work. Generic marketing copy gives them nothing to quote. A page that explains "how much does X cost and what affects the price" is far more citable than one that says "we offer competitive pricing".

### 5. Earn mentions on the sites AI engines trust

When we run AI visibility checks for clients, the sources cited for category questions are usually review platforms, industry directories, comparison articles, news sites and community discussions. If your competitors appear on those pages and you do not, AI answers will reflect that. Digital PR, listings on reputable directories and genuine reviews all feed GEO.

### 6. Show that content is current and attributable

Add a visible author or team, a published date and a "last updated" date. Keep key pages fresh. Content that is clearly owned and maintained is easier for both people and machines to trust.

### 7. Consider an llms.txt file

`llms.txt` is a proposed convention: a plain text file at the root of your site that summarises who you are and lists your most important pages. It is not an official standard and support varies between AI companies, but it is quick to add and does no harm.

## How do you measure GEO?

There is no "AI Search Console" yet, so measurement combines a few signals:

- **Prompt tracking.** Keep a fixed list of 10 to 30 buyer questions and check regularly whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews mention you, how they describe you and which sources they cite.
- **Referral traffic.** In Google Analytics, watch for visits referred by domains such as `chatgpt.com`, `perplexity.ai`, `copilot.microsoft.com` and `gemini.google.com`.
- **Branded search demand.** If AI answers recommend you, more people search for your brand by name.

Because AI answers vary from one run to the next, look at trends across many questions rather than any single answer.
